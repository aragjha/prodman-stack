# Is payment reminders + a client payment portal the right Q1 2027 bet, and will it drive seat expansion?

*All Ledgerly figures below are SYNTHETIC demo data from idea.md. Market facts carry links.*

## The call
Build it, but reframe it. Automatic reminders and a client payment portal are **table stakes**, not
a growth feature: they are included free in QuickBooks, Xero, FreshBooks and Bill.com. So the bet
pays off on **seat expansion only if it is designed to pull more of each firm's own staff onto
Ledgerly** by killing the manual spreadsheet chase (that is literally what customer Q1 offered to
do). Sequence **reminders-with-controls first** (it carries the seat mechanism and the lighter
build), portal second. Confidence: **medium** on "build it", **low** that this feature alone hits
300 net new seats.

## What would change this call
The target is **300 net new seats, which is 3.75× last quarter's pace of 80 seats (1,760→1,840)**.
The test: by **mid-February 2027**, of firms that switch reminders on, do ≥25% add at least one seat
within 30 days? If the add-seat rate is flat, reminders are a retention feature, not an expansion
one, and the seat goal needs sales/packaging, not more product. (Baseline "% of invoices paid late"
is unknown today — get it before committing; see Needs your judgment.)

## What you believed, and what happened to it
- **B1 — reminders/portal is the right bet because late payment is the top complaint:** *held, but
  downgraded.* It is the right thing to build, yet it is catch-up, not differentiation.
- **B2 — urgent because a competitor shipped reminders this summer:** *held.* Every major rival
  already has it; a standalone chaser ([Chaser](https://chaserhq.com/global/pricing)) charges
  $259–$1,169/month, so firms do pay to chase debt.
- **B3 (leadership) — this drives seat expansion:** *did not hold as stated.* Rivals bundle these
  features free and still bill per user; the money comes from more users on the platform, not from
  the feature. The seat lever is making Ledgerly the place the firm's staff do AR work.

**What it changes:** Because the features are commodity, Ledgerly cannot sell them as a paid add-on;
the return has to be *seats*. The only evidence-backed seat mechanism is Q1's: "I'd move our two new
juniors onto Ledgerly instead of the spreadsheet." That one sentence is the whole expansion thesis,
so reminders must be built to replace the manual chase, not just to send emails. And because of the
deliverability risk and Q3, firm-level controls (who gets chased, what it says, approval, stop when
paid) are a launch requirement, not a nice-to-have.

## What the PM books predict
- **Kano:** predicts these are "must-be" features — their absence loses deals, their presence earns
  little. The evidence agreed: rivals give them away. Treat as hygiene, not delight.
- **Jobs to be Done:** the job in Q1 is "stop me doing the Friday chase by hand," and the firm fires
  a spreadsheet to hire Ledgerly. The evidence agreed and pointed straight at seats.
- **Cagan's four risks:** value and feasibility look fine; **viability** (does it grow revenue?) is
  the open risk. The evidence flagged exactly that.
