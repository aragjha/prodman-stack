# Is payment reminders + a client payment portal the right Q1 2027 bet, and can it drive 300 net new seats?

## The call
Build it, but reframe why. Reminders are table stakes, not a wedge: a major competitor gives them away
free and built in ([Xero](https://trove.works/xero-invoice-reminders/)). The real opening is the control
those built-in tools lack, which is exactly what one of your own customers asked for. The seat-expansion
thesis is the weak part: it rests on a single customer quote, and 300 net new seats is roughly 3.75 times
last quarter's gain of 80. Evidence is medium for worth-building, low for drives-300-seats.

## What would change this call
Count how many firms in the base have a person doing manual chasing today (like the office manager who
said she'd "move our two new juniors onto Ledgerly" if reminders existed). If fewer than ~150 firms have
that pattern, the seat mechanism cannot reach 300 and the goal needs a second lever. Source: your own CRM
or a 20-firm sample Maya can pull this week.

## What you believed, and what happened to it
- **B1 — reminders + portal is worth building (late payment is the top complaint, a rival shipped it):**
  held, but narrowed. Worth building as a retention and get-paid-faster play; not a differentiator on its
  own because reminders are already free elsewhere.
- **B2 — this work drives firms to add seats:** still largely unproven. One firm named a concrete
  seat path; nothing in the evidence sizes that pattern across the base.

## What we found
| What we found | The evidence | How sure |
|---|---|---|
| Reminders are a free, built-in commodity in a major competitor | Xero's reminders are "free, built into Xero" ([Xero](https://trove.works/xero-invoice-reminders/)) | checked on the page |
| But built-in reminders are blunt: one schedule for all clients | "up to five automatic emails per invoice, on one schedule that applies to every customer" ([Xero](https://trove.works/xero-invoice-reminders/)) | checked on the page |
| Your customer wants exactly that missing control | "I'd need to control who gets chased and what it says" (firm admin, 15 seat firm) | from the PM |
| Payment portals earn on transaction fees, not seats | QuickBooks charges "2.99%" on card and "1%" on ACH with "No monthly fees or minimums" ([QuickBooks](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/payments/invoicing/)) | checked on the page |
| AR-chasing tools price by revenue band or per user, not per reminder | Chaser from $259/mo USD tiered by revenue ([Chaser](https://www.chaserhq.com/chaser-pricing)); BILL $49 per user/mo ([BILL](https://www.bill.com/product/pricing/)) | vendor pricing pages, one source each |
| Over-automating chasing has a real downside | "Following up after payment lands can damage trust" ([Chaser](https://www.chaserhq.com/blog/automated-payment-reminders)) | checked on the page |

**What it changes:** Reminders have to ship with per-customer control and tone settings, or you are
shipping a worse version of something firms can already get free. The portal's payoff is faster cash and
stickiness, not seat revenue, so do not pin the 300-seat goal on it. The only evidence-backed seat path is
removing manual chasing so a firm moves its spreadsheet staff onto Ledgerly seats.

## What the PM books predict
- **Jobs to be Done (Christensen):** predicts firms "hire" reminders to stop the weekly manual chase and
  "fire" the Friday spreadsheet routine; the office manager quote agrees, so the job is real.
- **Kano (needs customer answers, used only as a lens):** predicts reminders are now a basic expectation
  (free elsewhere) that stops losses rather than delighting; the free-everywhere evidence agreed.
- **The four big risks (Cagan):** value and feasibility look addressable; **viability** (does it move the
  seat number) is the open risk, and the evidence could not close it.
