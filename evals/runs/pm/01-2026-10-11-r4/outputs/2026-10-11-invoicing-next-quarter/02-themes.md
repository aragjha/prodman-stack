# Themes across the six interviews (B04 steps 3–5)

A theme needs three independent interviews to count as a real theme (PM.md section 3). Fewer is a
weak signal and is labelled so. With only six interviews these counts are directional signal, not
proportions — they do not tell you what share of the 300 firms feel this.

## Theme 1 — Getting clients to pay on time is painful (real theme)
**Explicitly asked for automatic reminders: 3 firms — A, E, F.**
- A: "Month end is hell. We chase clients for payment by email, one by one." — asked twice whether reminders could be automatic.
- E: "Automatic reminders would save my junior a day a month."
- F: uses a separate tool just to send payment reminders and pays for it; would drop it if reminders were built in.

**The broader "getting paid on time" pain shows in 5 of 6 firms — A, B, C, E, F** (only D does not
touch it). Beyond the 3 above:
- B: two clients paid late in September "because they lost the invoice email" — adjacent, but B did not ask for reminders.
- C: "I spend Friday afternoons copying payment status into a spreadsheet so I know who to chase." — the manual work of knowing who to chase.

So: automatic reminders is the one feature three firms asked for by name, sitting inside a
get-paid-on-time problem that five of six firms raised in some form.

## Weak signals (fewer than three interviews — do not build on these alone)
- **Payment-status tracking without a spreadsheet** — 1 firm (C). C would pay more for this. Close cousin of Theme 1; one voice.
- **Bank reconciliation** — 1 firm (E), and E itself called it "nice to have".
- **Dark mode** — 1 firm (B).

## Finding that goes against "reminders fixes it" (contradiction section)
- **Price / churn risk** — 1 firm (D, 2 seats): considering leaving for a cheaper tool, main complaint price per seat, did not mention reminders. Reminders would not address D. This is a separate problem (pricing / churn), and with one data point it is a weak signal too — but it is the one firm at risk of leaving, so it is worth watching, not building the quarter around.

## Segment pattern
- The firms feeling the chasing pain are the mid-to-larger ones by seat count: A (4), C (6), E (9), F (15). F, the largest here, already pays for an outside reminder tool.
- The one price-sensitive, at-risk firm is the smallest: D (2 seats).
- Direction only, from six firms: reminders demand appears to rise with the number of clients a firm juggles. This is a hypothesis to confirm, not a measured fact.
