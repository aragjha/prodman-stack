## The hole, in funnel order
1. **The promise** — 1 hour — you. Earliest gap, so everything below it is untestable. ~2,000 visits have now read a topic where a promise should be; you cannot attribute the 0.1% conversion to the checkout until the page says what changes for the reader.
2. **The capture** — 1.5 hours — you. This is where the money is leaving. Five weeks × ~400 visits ≈ 2,000 people have arrived, and you cannot contact one of them. Every post you have written spent its traffic once. **Fixing the checkout first would be fixing the wrong end of the leak** — the checkout is the one item with a real transaction behind it; the capture is the one with an assumption behind it.
3. **The proof** — 2 hours — you. 400 visits a week with no checkable claim is why people read and leave.
4. **The room** — 0.5 hours — you.
5. **The signal** — 1 hour — you. Until this exists you will keep rebuilding things on hunches. You believe people drop at the card step; you have no event that tells you whether anyone reaches the card step at all.

Total: **6 hours.** Sequence 1 → 2 → 5 (signal) before any new distribution, because pushing harder on distribution into a capture you have not verified multiplies the loss rather than the revenue.

## AFTER (not launch blockers)
- **The one-page checkout rebuild.** Which of the eight does it improve? Item 6 — already ✅, verified twice with real cards. It improves none of the five that are missing. This was your question and this is the answer: it is not a launch blocker, and it is not where your 2,000 lost visitors went. If the signal later shows a card-step drop among people who *reach* it, reopen this with data.
- Pushing harder on distribution. Blocked, not deferred: more traffic into an unverified capture form is more traffic spent once.

## The signal
We will know it worked when **emails captured per 100 landing-page visits** does **rise from unknown to 5 or more** by **2026-10-16**.

At ~400 visits a week, that is ~40 contactable people in two weeks against the 0 you can name today. If the promise and capture are fixed and that number stays near zero, the problem is the page and not the checkout — and you will finally have evidence instead of a hunch.
