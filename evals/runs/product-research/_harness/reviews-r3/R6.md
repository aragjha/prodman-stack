# R6 — Belief column + Insight belief consequence

Method: a script (`../r6extract.py`) parsed every pipe row between `## 2` and `## 3` of each MAP.md (all rows have 6 columns, Belief = col 5) and every `**Insight:**` line. Table cells checked: run-01 32, run-02 26, run-03 30, run-04 35, run-05 31 (154 total). No section-2 angle was cut, so nothing is exempt.

## Observations
- **Every table cell passes in all five runs.** No dashes, blanks, two-verdict cells, quality words or consequences anywhere in the Belief column. Both failures are in the Insight lines' `**Belief:**` field, which I held to the same one-verdict rule. If R6 is read narrowly (Insight lines only have to *state* a belief consequence), all five pass. That reading is too loose: "confirms B1 on demand; contradicts B1 on trust" states a verdict but cannot be acted on.
- **Where it breaks:** a run splits a belief mid-Insight along an axis it never defined in section 0 (run-02 "direction/magnitude", run-03 "on demand/on trust", "B2-usage/B2-revenue"). The B1/B2 split is allowed because it is declared up front. Inventing new axes on the fly gets around the one-verdict rule.
- **Table-to-Insight drift:** in run-03 angle 3 the table has 2 confirms, 3 contradicts and 1 unknown for B1, and the Insight reports both verdicts instead of choosing one. In run-02 angle 6 (2 confirms, 1 contradicts) the Insight attaches the dissent to the verdict. The rows are disciplined, but the summary line is where the hedging comes back.
- **Hidden attribution:** run-02 and run-05 put the B1/B2 attribution in the "Also changes" column, not the Belief cell (run-05 even says "Each row says which one it judges"). That is legal under R6, but the belief a row judges is not visible in the Belief column itself.
- Quality qualifiers next to a verdict ("weak", "weakly") appear in runs 02, 04 and 05. They are not failures because a verdict is present, but they are the first step toward the banned quality-word-in-place-of-a-verdict pattern.
