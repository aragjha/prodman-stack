Stopping before the first search. No decision is attached, and this skill does not research without one.

"I'll figure out what to do with it after" is the run it exists to refuse. A full-picture market map with no decision behind it cannot be wrong, so it gets filed under *interesting*, the call gets made on the three anecdotes you already have, and the map gets cited afterwards as support. "Too much rather than too little" on AI tools for PMs is also unbounded: hundreds of tools, eight angles, a week of reading, no verdict.

One ask, then I run. Four lines:

1. **Decision** — what will you choose differently depending on what comes back? Build · buy · position · price · enter · kill. Name the thing being decided.
2. **Goal it serves** — which of your own numbers has to move, and by how much for it to matter. Yours, not the web's.
3. **Belief** — what you currently think is true about this market, in one sentence a stranger could disagree with.
4. **Falsifier** — the finding that would make you drop that belief. A belief that cannot lose is a conclusion waiting for footnotes.

Optional: what you have already seen (so the run does not re-find it), and sources you trust or distrust.

Two shortcuts, if either fits:

- **The decision is already made and you want the evidence.** Say so. The map will still carry everything that contradicts the belief, in its own section, so you can choose what goes on the slide.
- **There genuinely is no decision yet.** Say that. I will give you the eight-angle map with the search keys written out, and nothing below it. You cut it to the three angles worth paying for, and we run those.
