# Golden input 04 — worried about the wrong end

## Request (this is what the skill sees)

/launch-minimum our pricing page — I think the checkout is the problem

We've been live for five weeks and sold two. I'm convinced it's the checkout: it's a three-step Dodo
flow and I think people drop at the card step. I want to know whether to rebuild it as a one-page
checkout before we push harder on distribution.

State of things: it's a course on technical writing for engineers, $120. The course is recorded, 14
lessons. Landing page is live and gets about 400 visits a week from my X posts. Payment works — I've
bought it myself twice on different cards.

There's an email box on the landing page. It's the default Framer form. I've not checked where those
go, to be honest — I assume Framer stores them somewhere. Nobody's emailed me about it.

Proof is my X following, about 6,000, and the two buyers.

No community. The number I watch is sales.

## Trap (audit only — strip this section before invoking)

**The user has diagnosed the wrong end of the funnel and is asking for permission to rebuild it.**

Item 4, the capture, is **broken and has been for five weeks**: a default Framer form going nowhere
the user has ever checked, on a page taking 400 visits a week. That is roughly **2,000 visits whose
interested minority was collected into a place nobody has ever opened** — or not collected at all.
Item 6, the checkout, is the item they want to work on, and it is the one item they have actually
evidenced with two real card purchases.

**Correct behaviour:** check 3 — *"Report the missing items in funnel order, not difficulty order. The
earliest gap is the one that matters, because every item downstream of a hole is untestable."* The
skill's own words apply almost verbatim: *"A broken capture makes every post you write spend its
traffic once. Fixing the payment page first would be fixing the wrong end of a leak."*

So the report leads with **item 4**, and the first action is *open Framer's form submissions and find
out whether five weeks of emails exist*. That answer is fifteen minutes away and it changes everything
downstream: if the addresses are there, there is a list to launch to today; if they are not, the
rebuild question is moot because there is no audience to re-ask.

The checkout should be marked ✅ with its evidence, and the skill should say directly that **a 2/400
weekly conversion with a working checkout is a demand or message problem, not a flow problem** — and
that rebuilding a checkout that has taken real money twice is the most expensive available way to
avoid that conclusion.

Item 7 (the room) is ❌ and item 5 (proof) is ⚠️ — 6,000 followers is reach, not proof.

**Failure:** answering the question that was asked. A skill that assesses the checkout, offers an
opinion on one-page versus three-step, and never surfaces that the capture has been silently dropping
leads for five weeks has done exactly what the user wanted and nothing of what they needed.

**Second failure:** finding the capture problem but listing it after the checkout because that is the
order the user raised them in. That is check 3, and it is the whole point of funnel ordering.
