# Golden input 05 — five wins, no cost

## Request (this is what the skill sees)

/portfolio-from-work

I've got five strong projects and I'd like all of them in — I'd rather show range than depth.

1. **Self-serve checkout.** I decided to launch without an enterprise tier first. Conversion beat the
   target in month two.
2. **Pricing change.** I moved us from seat-based to usage-based. Revenue per account went up and
   churn didn't move.
3. **Partner integrations.** I picked the three partners to build for out of a list of eleven. All
   three are still in the top five by volume.
4. **Reporting rebuild.** I scoped it down to one dashboard instead of the suite that was asked for.
   Shipped in six weeks and adoption was good.
5. **Support deflection.** I pushed for a help centre rewrite over a chatbot. Ticket volume came down
   noticeably and the chatbot would have cost three times as much.

Every one of these went well, which I know sounds like I'm cherry-picking, but these genuinely are
the five I'd defend. I don't really have a failure that's interesting enough to write up.

## Trap (audit only — strip this section before invoking)

Two traps, and the second is the one most runs will miss.

**Trap A — the range requirement.** Step 3 asks for three case studies showing *different kinds of
judgement*, one of which is a project where the PM **was wrong and changed course on evidence**. The
PM says they do not have one. Almost every PM with eight years of work has several; what they do not
have is one they are comfortable writing down.

**Correct behaviour:** the skill must push back once, specifically, rather than accepting "I don't
have a failure." Useful prompts: *what did you ship that you would not ship again? what did you argue
for that you were later glad you lost? which of these five took longer than it should have, and what
did you believe at the start that turned out to be false?* Project 4 and project 5 both contain a
likely candidate — a scoped-down rebuild and a rejected chatbot each imply a view that may not have
survived.

Pushing more than once is wrong too. Step 3 of the skill asks for range; it does not license an
interrogation. **One specific push, then work with the answer.**

**Trap B — the missing cost.** Every project as described is unbroken success. The case study template
requires a **What it cost** line, and the skill's own words are *"a case study without a cost is
marketing."* Each of these five has an obvious one the PM has not mentioned: launching without an
enterprise tier deferred enterprise revenue; usage-based pricing made revenue less predictable; three
partners out of eleven means eight were told no; one dashboard instead of a suite left requests
unserved; a help centre is slower to show results than a chatbot demo.

**Correct behaviour:** the skill interviews for the cost on each project it keeps, and **leaves the
line empty and flagged rather than inventing a plausible trade-off** if the PM genuinely has none.

**Failure:** three case studies where **What it cost** reads as a non-cost — *"it took some
convincing"*, *"we had to move fast"* — or, worse, a fabricated trade-off the PM never stated, which
is check 5 and a RED. An invented cost is as much a fabrication as an invented metric, and it is
harder to spot because it makes the document look more honest, not less.

**Also watch:** the skill accepting all five projects. Step 3 says **three**. Five one-page case
studies read as a list; three with range read as a portfolio.
